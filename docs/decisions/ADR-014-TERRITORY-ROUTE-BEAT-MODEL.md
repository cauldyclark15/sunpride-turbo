# ADR-014: Territory, route/beat, and outlet assignment model (ARCH-011)

Status: accepted (2026-09-25, JC lead) — provisional items marked inline

## Context

The client standardizes execution across territories and requires a monthly plan of stores to visit; its Job Evaluation Report observes calls in route order (`docs/requirements/SALES_OPS_STANDARDS_MEMO_2026-01-20.md` §§1, 3, 7, Annex G). The blueprint proposes territory → route/beat → MCP → planned visit, with outlets distinct from SAP customers (`docs/architecture/SFA_PLATFORM_BLUEPRINT.md` §§3–5). ADR-005 owns organizational scope; ADR-011 owns the configurable hierarchy. This ADR does not make hierarchy level names or map geometry authorization rules.

## Decision

- **Territory** is a stable operational selling area with code, name, channel, optional boundary polygon, status and an effective-dated owning `orgUnitId`. It is not itself an `orgUnits` node or a hard-coded hierarchy level. The owner may be any configured unit; an active territory has exactly one owner at a time, and ownership intervals cannot overlap. Scope follows the owner unit and its ancestor subtree under ADR-005 and ADR-011, resolved for the relevant date. A boundary is advisory for mapping and exception review, never proof of permission or proof that an outlet belongs there (blueprint §§3, 5; ADR-005).
- **Route and beat are one entity**: an identifiable, repeatable set of outlet stops within one territory, with status and optional weekday/cycle template. Route identity persists when its schedule or membership changes; route code is unique within its territory. A route belongs to only one territory at a time; moving it to another closes its old territorial association and opens an effective-dated association, rather than rewriting history. A route/beat is a sales coverage template, not a van `truckRouteSession` or a journey/stock-custody record (blueprint §§3–4; `packages/backend/convex/schema.ts` `truckRouteSessions`).
- **Ordered stops** are effective-dated route–outlet memberships with a positive integer sequence, preferred day/cycle, and optional visit frequency/window. Within a route and date, sequence positions are unique; reordering creates a new effective interval or revision, not a silent rewrite. One outlet has one primary territory and primary route at a time. Reassignment closes the previous interval and begins the next without deleting the outlet, historical visits, or earlier MCP references. A route's active stops must belong to its territory. Multiple visits in a month are generated by the MCP's dates and frequency, not by duplicating the outlet master (blueprint §§3–5).
- **Assignments are temporal, not just `active` flags.** Record effective-from/to (half-open intervals), actor, timestamp and reason for territory ownership, route territory, outlet primary territory/route, and salesperson route responsibility. Reject overlapping primary assignments for the same subject/date; prohibit retroactive changes to an approved plan's already elapsed dates. Preserve immutable IDs and prior assignment intervals so historical coverage resolves as-of execution date, not by current route membership. A salesperson can cover several routes, but one primary owner of each route/date is the recommended default. **Provisional — pending client confirmation:** shared route ownership and cross-territory outlet coverage may require explicit secondary assignments; neither is inferred from geography (blueprint §§3–4; ADR-005).
- **MCP is the scheduling authority.** Draft planners may use the route sequence and routine as defaults; approval snapshots outlet, territory, route, stop order and salesperson for each planned date. A later route reassignment or reordering does not silently change approved planned visits. Future changes to approved coverage use ADR-015's revised MCP and explicit replacement of future planned visits; executed and past visits retain their original references (blueprint §§4, 20; memo §3 and Annex A).

## Consequences

- Add scoped territory, route/beat and effective-dated ownership/membership/assignment tables, interval and sequence uniqueness checks, as-of readers, and audited reassignment mutations (`CVX-005`, `CVX-006`, `CVX-007`, `WEB-004`, `WEB-005`, `WEB-008`). Route/outlet queries and writes must enforce `orgUnitId` scope server-side; moving across scopes requires an authorized actor for both sides (ADR-005).
- `customers.territory` and `salesAssignments.{territory,active}` are legacy strings/flags, not historical assignment authority; add an operational outlet entity and migrate references without erasing old visits (`packages/backend/convex/schema.ts` `customers`, `salesAssignments`, `visits`; blueprint §5). Keep truck route sessions separate.

## Open questions

- Which actual territory and route codes, boundaries, cycle lengths and outlet-to-route mappings should be imported? Obtain current sheets under `ARCH-002` and outlet samples under `ARCH-005`; no geometry or cadence is claimed as a client fact.
- Does Sunpride allow simultaneous shared-route ownership or cross-territory coverage, and who resolves it? **Provisional — pending client confirmation.**
